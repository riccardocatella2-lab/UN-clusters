# 2–3 minute talk script

**Figure 1: biplot (`figures/06_biplot.png`), about 30 seconds**
"We combined three independent analyses of 10 UN indicators for 191 countries. We dropped the education variables, which were 63% missing, and the 16 countries that were mostly empty. We tested whether the gaps were random, and they weren't: rich countries don't report illiteracy and poor ones don't report employment. So we filled them using each country's other indicators instead of a median. PCA shows one dominant axis: PC1 explains 67% of the variance, and health, fertility, literacy and income all move together. Development is a bundle. PC2, at 12%, is almost entirely women's participation in work. Four components keep 92% of the information, so we clustered on those."

**Figure 2: choosing k (`figures/08_choosing_k.png`) and k = 2/3/4 maps (`figures/09_pca_k2_k3_k4.png`), about 45 seconds**
"We started with k = 2. To be honest, silhouette is highest at 2, and all three of our drafts found that. But look at what k = 2 is: 59 high-mortality countries against 132 others. The 'developed' group includes Switzerland and Tajikistan, a 350-times income range. It cuts off a tail, not two worlds. The elbow bends at 4 to 5. Structure captured goes from 56% with two clusters to 72% with four. Stability holds up to four and then drops. So we chose four. One thing we learned from comparing drafts: the middle boundaries are soft. For example, logging illiteracy moves ex-Soviet countries into their own cluster. The two extremes barely move."

**Figure 3: four worlds (`figures/14_four_worlds_representatives.png`), about 60 seconds**
"One country per cluster, each among the five closest to its cluster centre:
- **Ethiopia**: 7 children per woman, infant mortality over 100, under \$1 a day. Haiti, Nepal and Afghanistan are here too, so this cluster is about living conditions, not Africa.
- **India**: fertility is falling, but child mortality is still high and few women work or read. Surprise: Saudi Arabia is here, rich but with the same family and gender profile.
- **Colombia**: the big middle. Fertility near replacement, good literacy, modest income. It sits alongside China, Brazil and Russia, and even Qatar.
- **Portugal**: low fertility, long lives, high income. Surprise: Cuba is here, with a fraction of the income, because its health matches the rich world."

**Close, about 15 seconds**
"So the data say the world isn't two groups. It's a continuum where most countries sit in the middle, and four levels describe it far better, just as Rosling argues in *Factfulness*. The honest caveat is that on a continuum, exactly where you draw the lines depends on your choices."
